# LAWN HOGS

## Website Design & Development Specification

**Version:** 1.0
**Brand:** Lawn Hogs
**Positioning:** Parody premium lawn-care brand
**Brand line:** **Premium Lugged Outdoors™**
**Primary slogan:** **WE CUT GRASS. YOU TOUCH GRASS.**

---

# 1. Project Vision

The Lawn Hogs website should look like an absurdly well-funded outdoor lifestyle brand accidentally decided to mow lawns.

The joke is not that the website looks cheap.

The joke works because **everything looks exceptionally polished, confident, rugged, and expensive while the copy takes lawn mowing far more seriously than any reasonable person would.**

Visually, imagine the production values of a premium outdoor-equipment company combined with:

* suburban lawn obsession
* workwear culture
* exaggerated masculinity
* neighborhood rivalry
* pig jokes
* deadpan corporate branding
* self-aware advertising
* unnecessary swagger

The result should feel like a legitimate national brand at first glance.

Then the visitor starts reading.

---

# 2. Brand Promise

> **Your lawn will look so good, your neighbors will hate you.**

Lawn Hogs doesn't sell lawn mowing as a commodity.

It sells:

**Lawn dominance.**

The emotional payoff is the ridiculous satisfaction of looking across the street and knowing:

> Yeah. Ours looks better.

---

# 3. Brand Personality

The website should consistently communicate four traits.

### BOLD

We stand out.

Headlines are oversized. Statements are confident. Pages should never feel timid or generically corporate.

### HARDWORKING

We show up.

Underneath the comedy is an actual sense of competence, reliability, equipment, work, and results.

### HILARIOUS

We crack jokes.

Humor should appear throughout the experience without making every sentence a punchline.

### UNAPOLOGETIC

We do it our way.

The brand shouldn't sound as though a marketing committee approved it.

---

# 4. Comedy Rule

The fundamental writing rule:

## Play the brand straight.

Do not constantly tell visitors that Lawn Hogs is funny.

Lawn Hogs believes its own mythology.

That is what makes it funny.

Bad:

> We're a hilarious lawn company that doesn't take ourselves too seriously!

Good:

> **WE DON'T MOW LAWNS.
> WE DOMINATE THEM.**

Bad:

> Check out our funny lawn services.

Good:

> **YOUR GRASS HAS BEEN WARNED.**

# 6. Typography

Typography is a major part of the personality.

## Display Headlines — HOG WILD

Use a very heavy condensed display face with slightly imperfect character.

Reference characteristics:

* tall
* condensed
* bold
* rugged
* poster-like
* slightly distressed

Headlines should often use ALL CAPS.

Examples:

# BIG LAWNS.

# BIGGER ATTITUDES.

# YOUR GRASS

# HAS BEEN WARNED.

# MOW MONEY.

# FEWER PROBLEMS.

---

## Subheadings — HOG SLAB

Use a strong slab-serif or sturdy display serif.

Purpose:

* contrast the condensed headline font
* create an old workwear / equipment-manual quality

Example:

**WE DON'T MOW LAWNS.
WE DOMINATE THEM.**

---

## Body — HOG SANS

Use a highly readable modern sans serif.

Suggested implementation:

**Inter**

Body copy should remain extremely readable despite the expressive headline system.

---

## Handwritten Accent

Introduce a marker-style handwritten face exclusively for:

* pink notes
* annotations
* jokes
* arrows
* corrections
* stickers

Example:

*P.S. WE ALSO DO EDGING.*
*(IT'S KIND OF OUR THING.)*

Never use handwriting for normal body copy.

# 36. Motion

Motion should feel physical.

Use:

* sliding typography
* stamped badges
* rolling marquees
* subtle image zoom
* mower-line reveals
* sticker placement animation
* grass wipe transitions

Avoid:

* excessive parallax
* bouncing UI
* constant animation
* motion required for comprehension

Respect:

`prefers-reduced-motion`

All essential information remains available without animation.

---

# 37. Responsive Design

The mobile version should not simply shrink desktop.

Mobile priorities:

1. headline
2. service proposition
3. quote CTA
4. proof
5. services
6. work
7. trust

Large display headlines may occupy nearly the full screen width.

Buttons should be comfortably tappable.

Navigation must remain straightforward.

Decorative textures should reduce on smaller devices.

---

# 38. Accessibility

The parody cannot interfere with usability.

Target:

**WCAG 2.2 AA**

Requirements:

* semantic HTML
* keyboard-accessible navigation
* visible focus states
* proper heading hierarchy
* meaningful image alt text
* labeled form fields
* sufficient contrast
* no pure white background required
* minimum practical body size around 16px
* comfortable line height
* reduced-motion support
* no information communicated solely through color
* no auto-playing audio
* decorative images hidden from assistive technology

Comedy should live in the copy and art direction—not in inaccessible interaction patterns.

# 40. Desktop Grid

Maximum content width:

**1440px**

Editorial content:

**1200–1320px**

Grid:

**12 columns**

Spacing should be generous.

Sections alternate among:

* Grass Clippings cream
* Hog Green
* Tire Black
* photography
* occasional Pig Pink

Avoid putting every section inside rounded cards.

The site should feel like **an outdoor magazine and advertising campaign**, not a SaaS dashboard.

---

# 41. Border Radius

Keep most elements relatively square.

Recommended:

Buttons: `4–6px`

Cards: `0–8px`

Images: often `0px`

Stickers: irregular

The brand should feel rugged rather than bubbly.

---

# 42. Design Principle

Every page should contain three layers.

### Layer 1 — Immediate

Beautiful, premium outdoor brand.

### Layer 2 — Functional

Clear lawn-care information and obvious actions.

### Layer 3 — Discovery

Small jokes, labels, annotations, mascot appearances, fake specifications, and unexpected copy.

This rewards visitors for exploring without making the website confusing.

---

# 43. Content Ratio

Aim for approximately:

**70% legitimate business communication**

**20% personality**

**10% complete nonsense**

That 10% is important.

Too little and Lawn Hogs becomes another landscaping website.

Too much and nobody trusts it with their yard.
