It happened on a Tuesday.

At 07:15, a flatbed trailer backed into Steve's driveway across the street. The tailgate dropped with a metallic clang that echoed across Sector 4. Off rolled a neon-orange, 24-horsepower twin-cylinder lawn tractor equipped with headlights, cruise control, and two oversized plastic cup holders.

Steve's entire front lawn is 42 feet wide by 38 feet deep.

### The Mathematics of Suburban Desperation
Let us examine the empirical specifications of Steve's acquisition:

* **Tractor Turning Radius:** 16 inches
* **Steve's Yard Width:** 504 inches
* **Time Required to Cross Lot:** Approximately 4.2 seconds
* **Time Required to Make 3-Point Turn Near Flowerbed:** Approximately 48 seconds

Steve has purchased a vehicle designed for rolling wheat fields in Nebraska and deployed it in a suburban cul-de-sac where he spends 85% of his mowing time reversing away from his own birdbath.

### Why Did Steve Do This?
This was not an agricultural decision. This was a psychological reaction.

For the past three months, Steve has watched the Lawn Hogs rig pull up to your curb. He has watched Mike lay down 45-degree diamond stripes that look like emerald velvet. He has watched Hank sweep your driveway clean at 215 MPH while sipping iced tea.

Steve felt powerless. And when a suburban homeowner feels powerless, they go to the big-box home improvement center with a financing card and buy machinery they cannot store in their garage.

### The Proper Retaliation
Do not laugh at Steve out loud. Maintain professional decorum. Simply step onto your porch with a cold beverage on Saturday morning, look across the street, and let your diamond stripes do the talking.
